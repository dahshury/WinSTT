Most speech-to-text apps fall into two camps: **cloud dictation services** that stream your
audio to a server for a monthly fee, and **local tools** that run on your machine. WinSTT is
firmly in the second camp — and aims to be one of the most capable local-first desktop options.

<Callout type="success" title="Your voice stays on your machine">
  Local transcription runs on your own hardware. History and saved audio follow your
  retention settings. Cloud transcription or cleanup sends the relevant input to your
  selected provider when you enable it; neither is required for local dictation.
</Callout>

## WinSTT vs. cloud dictation

The biggest decision is where transcription and cleanup run. Superwhisper and OpenWhispr
offer both local and cloud paths; compare the selected mode, not just the product name.
Cloud processing sends audio or text to a provider and may involve a subscription or API charges.

## WinSTT vs. other local tools

Local tools differ in workflow as well as model choice. Several now include contextual
cleanup, assistants, or meeting notes. These are useful alternatives, not interchangeable
dictation utilities. The references below were reviewed on October 11, 2026.

WinSTT now imports dictionary and snippet JSON/CSV files and exports vocabulary-only JSON
archives. Dictionary import also accepts VoiceInk's version 1 archive. Existing entries
are kept when a file contains duplicate terms or triggers.

## Why local-first
